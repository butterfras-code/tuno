# One Uno234 activity

## Interface and response modes

Uno234 uses one activity surface with a compact shared toolbar: Mode, Exercise preset, Next exercise, and passage summary. The toolbar remains accessible on Practice and Settings. Practice keeps a short counting hint near the score; longer explanations and performance guidance share a contextual ? help popover. On desktop, Clear and Check sit in the counting row beneath the time signature and final barline; narrow screens place them beneath the counts to keep targets separate. Duration lines are created and edited directly on the score, without a separate passage-line panel. The performance surface contains the Start/Count In/input pad and separate Stop/Calibrate buttons at its right edge. Count-in indicators appear inside the pad only during count-in, with a fixed pad height across states. Timing downloads live under review details; there is no separate start/retry or notation-wrap button. Exercise settings appear only on the Settings page, alongside writing presentation/counting preferences. The future-settings inventory is collapsed under Future options. Its New Exercise action uses the same lifecycle as Next exercise.

Write Counts uses the existing keyboard/touch count editor and independent count, duration-line and rest-parenthesis feedback. Tap Rhythms uses press/hold/release with Space or the performance pad, audible one-bar count-in, exercise metronome, tempo and localized onset/release/rest review. Both offers either response with no prerequisite; Perform this exercise is an optional transition from writing to capture. It uses the ordinary Start flow without requiring Check or a correct answer.

The score uses the available viewport width with stable one-, two-, or four-measure rows, never horizontal scrolling. Narrow screens stack measures; all four supported practice measures and an 80px performance pad fit together in a compact performance area. Starting an attempt brings the score into view once before count-in; pad focus and playback do not scroll or jump between measures. The optional Wrap notation control forces one measure per row.

The performance pad sits directly below the score in Tap Rhythms and below the count-entry rows in Both. Calibrate, beneath the pad, opens the existing Timing setup in a native modal dialog. Escape, Close timing setup, backdrop dismissal and mode changes cancel any active calibration and stop its scheduled audio; the previous session offset survives cancellation. Tempo appears at the score’s top left with a metronome icon and compact BPM field; the displayed pulse unit follows the meter. Metronome during exercise is a saved Performance preference on Settings. Both controls lock during capture.

After a completed attempt, two compact timelines show Expected and Received attacks continuously across the exercise, with one Expected label and one Received label for the entire exercise. Matched received hits are anchored to the expected note’s measure and displaced by their corrected onset difference, so a slightly early first attack does not jump across the notation’s barline spacing. Unmatched hits remain positioned by their exercise time. Their widths and musical spacing match the notation, including automatically wrapped systems. Tied continuations create no extra expected hit. Received markers use the evaluator’s input-specific session correction; original timestamps remain available in raw review and the evidence download. Missing/extra hits use the same accent as score feedback. Hits beyond the rendered score are clipped to its edge with their actual time retained in the marker title. Interrupted attempts retain evidence without graded timelines.

A mode change preserves the same canonical exercise object, exercise ID, shared score nodes, pending configuration, count symbols, saved duration/rest markings, count feedback, performance record and review. Incomplete count-entry drafts are canceled while saved answers remain. Write Counts feedback and answer overlays are hidden in Tap Rhythms. During performance in Both, written counts are hidden by default; Show written counts during performance explicitly reveals them read-only. Writing feedback is hidden during capture. Switching during count-in/recording cancels and retains interrupted raw evidence; switching during calibration stops it and preserves the prior offset. Mode selection remains available while common exercise controls lock.

Next exercise generates one passage from pending settings, replaces its identity and clears both responses/results. Selecting a different review preset follows the same replacement path. Meter, length, ingredients, rest spelling, ties and syncopation are pending until Next/New Exercise; invalid combinations explain why generation is blocked and retain the current score and answers. Mode changes, system-layout changes, tempo edits and writing presentation preferences do not generate music. Retry and Reset answer remain mode-specific.

## Shared ownership

Settings → Performance includes **How to play**: **Tap** — “Tap when each note starts.” (default for new or older preferences without this field), and **Tap + Hold** — “Hold each note for its length. Release for rests.” It applies to Tap Rhythms and Both. The choice saves as `playStyle: 'tap' | 'hold'` in the existing version-1 shared preference store; missing/invalid values independently default to Tap. It preserves exercise identity, pending configuration, writing and completed performance review. It locks during audio activation, count-in, capture and calibration. Every new attempt snapshots the choice and a detached evaluation policy before awaiting audio activation. Retry uses the current choice; completed review/timelines/downloads use that attempt's snapshot. Historical attempt JSON without a choice retains its original hold-oriented interpretation.

Expected/Received timelines retain attacks in both settings. Tap has no duration bars or release grading. Tap + Hold adds thin sounding-duration bars, release endpoints and actual corrected hold intervals, split at measure/system breaks without continuation attacks. Accepted articulation gaps remain visible. Unmatched holds and overholds retain their actual times; outside-score extents clip at the score edge. Incomplete input has a null release, a distinct dashed observed extent bounded by capture end, and the explanation “no release observed”; it has no release endpoint. Titles, accessible descriptions and written review identify errors as well as color. No live sustain animation is added. See [evaluation rules and provisional numbers](performance-prototype.md#selectable-tap--tap--hold).

[Activity and preferences](../../src/apps/rhythm-walk/activity.ts) own the identity, configuration defaults, validated preference decoding, unified preset catalogue and replacement/generation boundary. The [shared directory](../../src/apps/rhythm-walk/shared/generation.ts) contains the existing bounded generator, review fixtures, meter/rest policies, notation renderer, settings component and settings inventory. Historical Count Writing import paths re-export these implementations for existing consumers; they contain no copied schemas or engine logic.

[The shell](../../src/apps/rhythm-walk/main.ts) renders shared controls and the score once. Measure editors retain their saved responses without being recreated on a mode switch. The same renderer supplies notation and ties in every mode. The shell projects performance review onto its written source references and removes those annotations when returning to writing.

Write Counts owns its answer grid, input/editor, presentation and count evaluation. [The performance component](../../src/apps/rhythm-walk/performance.ts) owns tempo/metronome, audio activation, count-in/capture transport, calibration and performance review. Both evaluators consume the current canonical passage. Capture snapshots that passage and derives timing through `derivePassage` and the shared meter's explicit pulse. There is no second authored timing representation and no performance-only engraver. Exact written fractions become seconds only at the audio scheduling boundary.

The shared configuration retains 4/4 and two measures as defaults, all four supported meters, 1–4 measures, all enabled notes/rests, ties/syncopation, rest spelling and every prior review fixture. Review presets are musical examples, not approved difficulty or UIL presets. The existing generator's validation and bounded draws remain intact. 6/8 retains six required written numbers, grouping 123 / 456, and two dotted-quarter performance pulses; BPM and count-in labels follow the performance pulse, independent of written counts.

## Entry points and persistence

Both `/prototypes/rhythm-walk/` and `/prototypes/count-writing/` are compatible entry points for one bundled implementation. The legacy count URL references the canonical JS/CSS; query parameters such as `?fixture=six-bar-tie` and `#settings` remain supported. `npm run dev:walk` and `npm run dev:counts` serve the same activity and both paths. No production suite navigation, service worker or portable rhythm release was introduced.

`localStorage['tuno.rhythm-walk.preferences.v1']` stores shared generation settings, selected preset, wrap preference, response mode, count presentation/convention/offbeat preferences, tempo, metronome and optional visible counts. They survive reloads and navigation between entry points on the same origin. Explicit valid `?fixture=` takes precedence over the stored preset; otherwise saved valid fields take precedence over defaults. Malformed/version-mismatched data uses defaults; invalid individual fields fall back independently. Structurally valid but unfillable ingredient settings remain editable and block Next. On reload with such settings, an initial default passage is shown while pending invalid settings remain visible; no unsupported music is generated. Responses/results are exercise-local memory and reset on reload; changing modes keeps them.

The legacy count prototype had no persisted preference store, so there is no competing count-storage key to migrate. Existing session offsets retain `sessionStorage['tuno.rhythm-walk.timing.v1']` and its validation unchanged. Shared preferences never overwrite calibration. Timing values last for the tab session, including reloads; automatic estimation does not refit an already saved offset, manual tap-along can explicitly replace it, and other input methods remain uncorrected. Offset evidence, original timestamps and raw/corrected evaluation are preserved. Storage denial falls back to usable memory.

Count-in uses whole bars of the first measure with at least four **performance beats**: four clicks in 4/4; two bars and six clicks in 3/4; two bars and four clicks in 2/4 or 6/8. The 6/8 beat remains a dotted quarter, independent of the six written counts. A reserved row above the first measure shows one numbered dot for every count-in click, restarting beat numbers at each bar. Each dot lights and pulses from the same audio-clock plan that schedules its click; the final two dots carry Ready and Go. The exercise starts on the following beat. The row remains in place during capture and review to prevent layout jumps; cancellation clears the active dot. Write Counts alone hides this performance cue. Reduced-motion preferences retain lighting without animation.

Time signatures are a shared checkbox pool, defaulting to 4/4 only. Next/New Exercise draws one selected meter uniformly and uses it throughout the entire passage; there are no mixed-meter exercises. Checking a simple meter also enables quarter notes; checking 6/8 enables eighth notes. Existing ingredient selections stay checked. The generator draws only compatible, reachable ingredients under the chosen meter, rest-notation and syncopation policies. Half notes/rests are omitted from generated 6/8 exercises, including when unintuitive notation is enabled. Dotted-quarter rests in simple meters are skipped unless unintuitive notation is enabled. Unreachable optional ties/syncopations do not block a fillable passage. Empty meter/ingredient pools or a selected meter with no complete-bar solution still explain the problem and block Next. Editing these settings preserves the current score and both responses until Next.

Saved `generation.meters` retains the pool across reloads and entry points. Legacy `generation.meter` migrates to a one-item pool; the new array wins if both exist. Duplicate meters are removed, malformed pools fall back to defaults, and an empty pool stays editable. This supersedes older strict ingredient-reachability errors and single-meter settings descriptions.
